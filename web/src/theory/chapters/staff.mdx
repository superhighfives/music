<p class="lede">The keyboard shows notes side by side. Written music stacks them up instead: higher notes sit higher on the page. Once you can read that page, you can read a melody, a chord or a whole piano part, and every figure in this book becomes something you could play.</p>

<Section id="lines-and-spaces" title="Five lines, four spaces">
  <p>Music is written on a <Term>staff</Term>: five horizontal lines with four spaces between them. A note can sit <i>on</i> a line, with the line running through its middle, or <i>in</i> a space, between two lines.</p>
  <Staff client:visible
    items={[
      { notes: ['E4'], label: 'line' },
      { notes: ['F4'], label: 'space' },
      { notes: ['G4'], label: 'line' },
      { notes: ['A4'], label: 'space' },
      { notes: ['B4'], label: 'line' },
    ]}
    label="Notes going up the staff, alternating line, space, line, space, line"
    caption="Each step up goes from a line to the next space, or a space to the next line."
  />
  <p>Moving up one line or space is moving up one letter: C to D, D to E. So the staff is a ladder of the white keys, and reading it is mostly counting rungs.</p>
  <Big>Line, space, line, space: each step up the staff is the next letter.</Big>
</Section>

<Section id="treble" title="The treble clef">
  <p>On its own, a staff doesn't say which note is which. A <Term>clef</Term> at the start fixes that. The <Term>treble clef</Term>, also called the G clef, curls around the second line from the bottom and marks it as <b>G4</b>, the G above middle C.</p>
  <Staff client:visible
    items={[{ notes: ['G4'], tone: 'lit', label: 'G4' }]}
    label="Treble clef with G4 on the second line, where the clef curls"
    caption="The treble clef's curl wraps round the G line."
  />
  <p>From G you can count to anything: one step up is A in the space above, one step down is F in the space below. Here are the lines and spaces of the treble staff, bottom to top.</p>
  <Staff client:visible items="E4 F4 G4 A4 B4 C5 D5 E5 F5" names label="Every line and space of the treble staff, E4 up to F5, named" />
  <Aside label="Mnemonics">The lines, bottom to top, are E G B D F: <b>Every Good Boy Does Fine</b>. The spaces spell <b>FACE</b>. They're handy at first, but don't stop there; see the section on landmarks below.</Aside>
  <p>The treble clef is used for higher sounds: the right hand on a piano, voices, guitar, violin, flute.</p>
</Section>

<Section id="bass" title="The bass clef">
  <p>The <Term>bass clef</Term>, or F clef, is for lower sounds: the piano's left hand, bass guitar, cello. Its two dots sit either side of the fourth line, and mark it as <b>F3</b>, the F below middle C.</p>
  <Staff client:visible
    clef="bass"
    items={[{ notes: ['F3'], tone: 'lit', label: 'F3' }]}
    label="Bass clef with F3 on the fourth line, between the clef's two dots"
    caption="The bass clef's dots sit either side of the F line."
  />
  <Staff client:visible clef="bass" items="G2 A2 B2 C3 D3 E3 F3 G3 A3" names label="Every line and space of the bass staff, G2 up to A3, named" />
  <Aside label="Mnemonics">Bass lines, bottom to top: G B D F A, <b>Good Boys Do Fine Always</b>. Bass spaces: A C E G, <b>All Cows Eat Grass</b>.</Aside>
</Section>

<Section id="ledger" title="Ledger lines and middle C">
  <p>Notes can go above or below the staff. Short extra lines, called <Term>ledger lines</Term>, extend the staff just for that note. The most important one is <b>middle C</b>: one ledger line below the treble staff.</p>
  <Staff client:visible
    items={[
      { notes: ['A3'] },
      { notes: ['B3'] },
      { notes: ['C4'], tone: 'lit', label: 'C4' },
      { notes: ['D4'] },
      { bar: true },
      { notes: ['G5'] },
      { notes: ['A5'], label: 'A5' },
      { notes: ['C6'], label: 'C6' },
    ]}
    label="Notes below and above the treble staff on ledger lines, with middle C lit"
    caption="Middle C sits on one ledger line below the treble staff. Above, A5 takes one ledger line and C6 two."
  />
  <p>Ledger lines follow the same rule as the staff: line, space, line. Count them the way you'd count the lines of the staff, and the note's letter follows.</p>
</Section>

<Section id="grand" title="The grand staff">
  <p>Piano music joins a treble staff and a bass staff with a brace into a <Term>grand staff</Term>. Middle C sits between them: one ledger line below the treble staff, or one ledger line above the bass staff. Same key, same sound, written in whichever staff is nearer.</p>
  <Staff client:visible
    clef="grand"
    items={[
      { notes: ['C4'], tone: 'lit', label: 'C4', staff: 'treble' },
      { notes: ['C4'], tone: 'lit', label: 'C4', staff: 'bass' },

    label="A grand staff: middle C written in the treble staff and again in the bass staff, then a C chord spread across both"

</Section>

<Section id="landmarks" title="Read by landmarks">
  <p>Mnemonics work, but spelling out every note from the bottom line is slow. Fluent readers learn a handful of <b>landmark notes</b> by sight, then count steps from the nearest one.</p>
  <Staff client:visible
    clef="grand"
    items={[

      { notes: ['G4'], tone: 'cool', label: 'G4' },
      { notes: ['C5'], tone: 'cool', label: 'C5' },
    ]}
    label="Landmark notes on the grand staff: bass C3 and F3, middle C, treble G4 and C5"

  />
  <p>To read any note, find the nearest landmark and count: a line to the next space is one letter. D5 is one step above the C5 landmark; E3 is one step below F3. Before long you won't count at all; you'll just see them.</p>

<Section id="accidentals" title="Sharps and flats on the staff">
  <p>An accidental is written just <i>before</i> the note it changes, on the same line or space. The note stays in the same place; the sign moves it up or down a half step.</p>
  <Staff client:visible items="F#4 Bb4 C#5 Eb5" names label="F sharp, B flat, C sharp and E flat on the treble staff, each with its sign in front" />
  <p>Within a bar, an accidental lasts until the bar line, so a second F in the same bar is still F♯ unless a natural ♮ cancels it. The next bar starts fresh.</p>
  <Staff client:visible
    items={[{ notes: ['F#4'], label: 'F♯' }, { notes: ['F#4'], label: 'still F♯' }, { notes: ['F4'], tone: 'lit', label: 'F♮' }]}
    label="F sharp, then F sharp again without a sign shown, then F natural with a natural sign"
    caption="The figure draws every sign; on real sheet music the second F♯ would have none, because the first carries through the bar."
  />
</Section>
